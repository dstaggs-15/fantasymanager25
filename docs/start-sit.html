How the "Start Score" is Calculated (In-Depth)

The Start Score is a comprehensive rating from 0-10 that provides a data-driven recommendation for the upcoming week. It is a weighted average of four key factors, each normalized to a 0-10 scale.

1. Player Talent (40% Weight)

This measures a player's baseline skill. It's calculated using their season-long Points Per Game (PPG) and is normalized against the top-performing player at their same position. This means the #1 PPG player at a position gets a 10, and everyone else is scored relative to them.

Talent Score = (Player's PPG / Max PPG at Position) * 10

2. Weekly Matchup (30% Weight)

This grades the difficulty of the opponent. It's based on our defensive rankings, which calculate how many fantasy points each NFL team allows to each position (1st = easiest, 32nd = hardest). A better matchup results in a higher score.

Matchup Score = ((32 - Opponent's Rank) / 31) * 10

3. Offensive Line (15% Weight)

This scores the quality of a player's blocking. It uses our "homegrown" O-line rankings, which are generated by analyzing a team's run blocking (EPA per rush) and pass blocking (sack rate). A better O-line rank results in a higher score.

O-Line Score = ((32 - Team's O-Line Rank) / 31) * 10

4. Efficiency (15% Weight)

This measures how effective a player is with their opportunities and how well they protect the ball. The calculation is specific to the player's position:

  • For QBs: A weighted score combining Touchdown Rate, Yards Per Attempt, Interception Rate (negative), and Fumble Rate (negative).
  • For RBs, WRs & TEs: A weighted score combining Yards Per Touch (positive) and Fumble Rate (negative).

Each component is normalized against an "elite" benchmark to generate the final 0-10 score.

The Weighting Rationale

The weights are assigned based on their general importance to fantasy production:

  • Talent (40%): The highest weight is given to a player's proven ability to score points. Elite players can often overcome bad situations. This is the strongest predictor of performance.
  • Matchup (30%): The most important *weekly variable*. An elite matchup can elevate an average player, while a terrible matchup can limit a great one.
  • O-Line & Efficiency (15% each): These are critical "context" and "tie-breaker" metrics. They add crucial nuance to separate players who may have similar talent and matchups. A great O-line provides a higher ceiling, while high efficiency shows a player makes the most of their opportunities.
